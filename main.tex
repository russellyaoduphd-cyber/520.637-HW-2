\subsection*{a} 
There are $N$ stages, where stage $k$ corresponds to parking space
$k$, for $k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}$.
Let $F$ denote that the current parking space is free, $F^c$ denote
that it is taken, and $T$ denote the terminal state in which the driver
has already parked. Then
\[\mathcal S_0=\{F,F^c\},\]
\[\mathcal S_k=\{F,F^c,T\}, \qquad k=1,\ldots,N-1, \]
and after the last decision stage,
\[\mathcal S_N=\{T\}. \]
For $k=0,\ldots,N-1$, the action sets are
\[ \mathcal A_k(F)=\{\text{park},\text{continue}\}, \]
\[\mathcal A_k(F^c)=\{\text{continue}\}. \]
At the terminal state $T$, no further decision is required.
\subsection*{b}
Let $a^1$ denote ``park'' and $a^2$ denote ``continue''. Let $C_{k+1}$ denote the incurred cost.
we have:\[P(S_{k+1}=T, C_{k+1}=c(k)|S_k=F,A_k=a^1)=1 \quad k= 0,1,\dots,{N-1}\]
Then we consider the state-action pair with $a^2$, for:
\[k=0,1,\ldots,{N-2}\]
\[P(S_{k+1}=F, C_{k+1}=0|S_k=F, A_k=a^2)=p(k+1)\]
\[P(S_{k+1}=F^c, C_{k+1}=0|S_k=F, A_k=a^2)=1-p(k+1)\]
\[P(S_{k+1}=F, C_{k+1}=0|S_k=F^c, A_k=a^2)=p(k+1)\]
\[P(S_{k+1}=F^c, C_{k+1}=0|S_k=F^c, A_k=a^2)=1-p(k+1)\]
For $k=N-1$, if the driver continues, then
\[P(S_N=T, C_N=C|S_{N-1}=F,A_{N-1}=a^2)=1\]
\[P(S_N=T, C_N=C|S_{N-1}=F^c,A_{N-1}=a^2)=1\]
All other transition-cost probabilities are zero.
\subsection*{c}
\[J^{\star}_N(T)=0\]
for $k={N-1}$, \[J^{\star}_{N-1}(F)=\min \{c(N-1),C\}\]
\[J^{\star}_{N-1}(F^c)=C\]
\[J^{\star}_{N-1}(T)=0\]
for $k=0,1,\ldots, {N-2}$,

\[J^{\star}_{k}(T)=0 \quad k= 1,2,\ldots,{N-2}\]
These equations are evaluated backward for

\[J^\star = p(0)J_0^\star(F) + (1-p(0))J_0^\star(F^c). \]
\subsection*{d}
\begin{proof}
Define \[W^{\star}_{N-1}=J^{\star}_{N-1}(F^c)=C\]
Then \[J^{\star}_{N-1}(F)=\min \{c(N-1),W^{\star}_{N-1}\} \leq W^{\star}_{N-1}\]
\[J^{\star}_{N-2}(F^c)=p(N-1)J^{\star}_{N-1}(F) + (1-p(N-1)) J^{\star}_{N-1}(F^c)\leq W^{\star} _{N-1}\]
Define $W^{\star}_{N-2}=J^{\star}_{N-2}(F^c)$, we have $W^{\star}_{N-2} \leq W^{\star} _{N-1}$\\
For $k=0,1,\ldots, {N-2}$, define
\[W^{\star}_{k} = J^{\star}_{k}(F^c)\]
Then \[W^{\star}_{k} = p(k+1) \min \{c(k+1), W^{\star}_{k+1}\}+(1-p(k+1))W^{\star}_{k+1} \leq W^{\star}_{k+1}\leq W^{\star}_{N-1}=C\]
Hence $\{W^{\star}_k\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ is a non-decreasing sequence bounded by $C$.\\
Also, from the assumption, $\{c(k)\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ is non-increasing.\\
Moreover, since \[J_k^\star(F)=\min\{c(k),W_k^\star\}, \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\]
parking is an optimal action at a free space $k$ if and only if
\[c(k)\leq W_k^\star.\]
In the case of equality, both parking and continuing are optimal, and we choose to park.\\
There are two cases. First, suppose that there exists
$k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}$ such that
$c(k)\leq W_k^\star$. Define
\[K=\min\{k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}:c(k)\leq W_k^\star\}.\]
For every $k<K$, by the definition of $K$,
\[c(k)>W_k^\star,\]
so the driver should continue even if space $k$ is free.
For every $k\geq K$, since $c(k)$ is non-increasing and
$W_k^\star$ is non-decreasing,
\[c(k)\leq c(K)\leq W_K^\star\leq W_k^\star. \]
Thus, from space $K$ onward, the driver should park at the first free
space.
If no such $K$ exists, then $c(k)>W_k^\star$ for every
$k=0,\ldots,N-1$, so the driver should continue through all parking
spaces and park in the garage. This is the degenerate threshold case $K=N$.
\end{proof}
