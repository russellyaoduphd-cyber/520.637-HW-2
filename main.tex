There are $N$ stages, where stage $k$ corresponds to parking space
$k$, for $k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}$.
Let $F$ denote that the current parking space is free, $F^c$ denote
that it is taken, and $T$ denote the terminal state in which the driver
has already parked. Then
\[\mathcal S_0=\{F,F^c\},\]
\[\mathcal S_k=\{F,F^c,T\}, \qquad k=1,\ldots,N-1, \]
and after the last decision stage,
\[\mathcal S_N=\{T\}. \]
For $k=0,\ldots,N-1$, the action sets are
\[ \mathcal A_k(F)=\{\text{park},\text{continue}\}, \]
\[\mathcal A_k(F^c)=\{\text{continue}\}. \]
At the terminal state $T$, no further decision is required.

\subsection*{d}
\begin{proof}
Define \[W^{\star}_{N-1}=J^{\star}_{N-1}(F^c)=C\]
Then \[J^{\star}_{N-1}(F)=\min \{c(N-1),W^{\star}_{N-1}\} \leq W^{\star}_{N-1}\]
\[J^{\star}_{N-2}(F^c)=p(N-1)J^{\star}_{N-1}(F) + (1-p(N-1)) J^{\star}_{N-1}(F^c)\leq W^{\star} _{N-1}\]
Define $W^{\star}_{N-2}=J^{\star}_{N-2}(F^c)$, we have $W^{\star}_{N-2} \leq W^{\star} _{N-1}$\\
For $k=0,1,\ldots, {N-2}$, define
\[W^{\star}_{k} = J^{\star}_{k}(F^c)\]
Then \[W^{\star}_{k} = p(k+1) \min \{c(k+1), W^{\star}_{k+1}\}+(1-p(k+1))W^{\star}_{k+1} \leq W^{\star}_{k+1}\leq W^{\star}_{N-1}=C\]
Hence $\{W^{\star}_k\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ is a non-decreasing sequence bounded by $C$.\\
Also, from the assumption, $\{c(k)\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ is non-increasing.\\
Moreover, since \[J_k^\star(F)=\min\{c(k),W_k^\star\}, \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,N-1,\]
parking is an optimal action at a free space $k$ if and only if
\[c(k)\leq W_k^\star.\]
In the case of equality, both parking and continuing are optimal, and we choose to park.\\
There are two cases. First, suppose that there exists
$k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}$ such that
$c(k)\leq W_k^\star$. Define
\[K=\min\{k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}:c(k)\leq W_k^\star\}.\]
For every $k<K$, by the definition of $K$,
\[c(k)>W_k^\star,\]
so the driver should continue even if space $k$ is free.
For every $k\geq K$, since $c(k)$ is non-increasing and
$W_k^\star$ is non-decreasing,
\[c(k)\leq c(K)\leq W_K^\star\leq W_k^\star. \]
Thus, from space $K$ onward, the driver should park at the first free
space.
If no such $K$ exists, then $c(k)>W_k^\star$ for every
$k=0,\ldots,N-1$, so the driver should continue through all parking
spaces and park in the garage. This is the degenerate threshold case $K=N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{a}
We formulate the problem as a finite-horizon MDP with three decision stages,
$k=0,1,2$, corresponding to the three weeks. Let
\[\mathcal S=\{R,B\},\]
where $R$ denotes that the machine is running at the beginning of the week
and $B$ denotes that it is broken. The initial state is
\[S_0=R.\]
The available actions depend on the current state:
\[\mathcal A(R)=\{M,N\},\]
where $M$ denotes preventive maintenance and $N$ denotes no maintenance, and
\[\mathcal A(B)=\{\text{repair},\text{replace}\}.\]

We use the weekly net profit as the reward. The nonzero state-reward
transition probabilities are
\[
P(S_{k+1}=R,R_{k+1}=100\mid S_k=R,A_k=M)=0.6,
\]
\[
P(S_{k+1}=B,R_{k+1}=-20\mid S_k=R,A_k=M)=0.4,
\]
\[
P(S_{k+1}=R,R_{k+1}=120\mid S_k=R,A_k=N)=0.3,
\]
\[
P(S_{k+1}=B,R_{k+1}=0\mid S_k=R,A_k=N)=0.7,
\]
\[
P(S_{k+1}=R,R_{k+1}=60\mid S_k=B,A_k=\text{repair})=0.6,
\]
\[
P(S_{k+1}=B,R_{k+1}=-60\mid S_k=B,A_k=\text{repair})=0.4,
\]
and
\[
P(S_{k+1}=R,R_{k+1}=0\mid S_k=B,A_k=\text{replace})=1.
\]
All other state-reward transition probabilities are zero.

Since in part (a) we do not care whether the machine is running or broken
after the third week, the terminal rewards are
\[
J_3^\star(R)=J_3^\star(B)=0.
\]
We now apply backward induction.
For the third week, $k=2$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_2^\star(R)
&=\max\{0.6(100)+0.4(-20),\;0.3(120)+0.7(0)\}\\
&=\max\{52,36\}=52,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action in state $R$ is preventive maintenance. Also,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_2^\star(B)
&=\max\{0.6(60)+0.4(-60),\;0\}\\
&=\max\{12,0\}=12,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action in state $B$ is repair.
For the second week, $k=1$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_1^\star(R)
&=\max\{0.6(100+52)+0.4(-20+12),\\
&\qquad\qquad 0.3(120+52)+0.7(0+12)\}\\
&=\max\{88,60\}=88,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action in state $R$ is preventive maintenance. For state $B$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_1^\star(B)
&=\max\{0.6(60+52)+0.4(-60+12),\;0+52\}\\
&=\max\{48,52\}=52,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action is replacement.
For the first week, $k=0$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_0^\star(R)
&=\max\{0.6(100+88)+0.4(-20+52),\\
&\qquad\qquad 0.3(120+88)+0.7(0+52)\}\\
&=\max\{125.6,98.8\}=125.6,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action in state $R$ is preventive maintenance. For completeness,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_0^\star(B)
&=\max\{0.6(60+88)+0.4(-60+52),\;0+88\}\\
&=\max\{85.6,88\}=88,
\end{aligned}\]
so the optimal action in state $B$ is replacement.
Therefore, the optimal (deterministic) policy is
\[\begin{array}{c|cc}
& R & B\\ \hline
\text{Week 1} & \text{maintain} & \text{replace}\\
\text{Week 2} & \text{maintain} & \text{replace}\\
\text{Week 3} & \text{maintain} & \text{repair}
\end{array}\]
and since $S_0=R$, the maximum expected total profit over the three weeks is
\[J_0^\star(R)=125.6.\]
\subsection*{b}
With a penalty of $100$ if the machine is broken at the end of the third
week, the terminal rewards become
\[J_3^\star(R)=0, \qquad J_3^\star(B)=-100.\]
We only need to recompute the last stage, $k=2$. For state $R$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_2^\star(R)
&=\max\{0.6(100)+0.4(-20-100),\;
0.3(120)+0.7(0-100)\}\\
&=\max\{12,-34\}=12,
\end{aligned}\]
so preventive maintenance remains optimal.
For state $B$,
\[\begin{aligned}
J_2^\star(B)
&=\max\{0.6(60)+0.4(-60-100),\;0\}\\
&=\max\{-28,0\}=0,
\end{aligned} \]
so replacement is optimal.
Therefore, the optimal policy at the last stage is
\[\begin{array}{c|cc}
& R & B\\ \hline
\text{Week 3} & \text{maintain} & \text{replace}
\end{array} \]
Compared with part (a), the optimal action in state $R$ does not change,
while the optimal action in state $B$ changes from repair to replacement.
